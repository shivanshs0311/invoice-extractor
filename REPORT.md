# Invoice extraction pipeline: report

Shivansh Singh. AI intern assignment, Es Magico.

Every file in `outputs/` validates against the assignment's `schema.json`, checked by `scripts/preflight.py`. The results block in section 4 is generated by
`python eval.py --write-report` and is never typed by hand. All runs were made on 2026-11-21 with a free Google AI Studio key, so the real bill was $0.
Costs below are what the same tokens would cost at list price.

## 1. Summary

- **What it is.** A pipeline that reads a PDF or image and returns schema-valid JSON, checks that line items plus tax equal the total, retries once when a check
  fails, flags anything it is still unsure about, and returns an explicit error record instead of a guess when a file cannot be read.
- **Accuracy.** On the 10 labelled documents: 10 of 10 fully correct, 100% on every field, with the first version of the prompt (never edited after an eval run).
  I also checked all 10 unlabelled outputs against the source documents and found no wrong field. The pack is clean, so I added a stress set of damaged copies,
  where whole-document accuracy falls to 14 of 30 (mild 10 of 10, medium 4 of 10, severe 0 of 10, and the severe ones are now explicit failures, not guesses).
- **Cost.** $0.00165 per labelled document at list price (1,867 input and 437 output tokens on average), $0.00141 over all 20 documents. Billed cost: $0.
- **Biggest weakness.** Misread digits and characters on low-quality scans (invoice numbers, dates, tax and total). The arithmetic check cannot catch them when the
  model's errors cancel out, so these documents are caught only by the model's own "legibility: fair" rating.

## 2. Approach

### 2.1 Pipeline

```
file -> ingest -> LLM (JSON schema forced) -> schema check -> sanity + totals checks -> JSON
         |                  ^                                      |
         |                  +--- one retry with the failed checks --+
         |                       (stronger model if available, text + images)
         +- bad input? fail fast, no API call, error record
```

### 2.2 Model and input choice, and why

- **Models.** Gemini 3.5 Flash-Lite for the first pass ($0.30 input and $2.50 output per million tokens) and Gemini 3.5 Flash for the one retry ($1.50 and $9.00).
  Prices are stored in `pricing.json`. I took them from Google's announcements and pricing page as of 2026-11-21, some of it as quoted by third parties, so
  re-check before relying on them. Most invoices are easy, so the cheap model does the bulk of the work and the stronger model is only paid for when a check fails.
  Thinking tokens are billed as output tokens, and the cost figures include them. I also tried a newer Flash model (`gemini-3.8-flash`) on one document: it took 76 seconds and spent
  964 thinking tokens, which is too slow and costly for the first pass.
- **Free-tier reality.** On the free key the stronger model is limited to 20 requests per day (checked 2026-11-21), and its daily quota ran out during my runs.
  A daily quota error is not retried, because waiting cannot fix it. Instead the retry runs once on the first-pass model, still with the quoted check failures and
  the page images. So the stronger model's role as the **retry** was never exercised on a real failure (see 2.6). Its quality as a first-pass model was measured once, after the quota reset (see the ablation).
- **Input.** Digital PDFs are sent as a layout-preserving text layer, which gives exact digits for fewer tokens than images. Scans, photos and image-dominated PDFs
  are rendered to page images (EXIF rotation fixed, longest edge capped at 1600 px), because they have no usable text. `--mode text|vision|hybrid` forces one
  representation, and `scripts/ablation.py` compares them on the labelled set.
- **Output.** The model can only answer with JSON that matches a schema (`response_mime_type` plus `response_schema`), and Pydantic validates it again, because a
  schema-shaped answer can still hold a wrong value. No sampling parameters are sent. SDK retries are off and my own loop counts and prints every retry.

### 2.3 Prompting approach

One system prompt, version `v1` (`invoice_extractor/prompts.py`, hash printed in every run header). It does five things:

1. Defines what counts as an invoice and lists what does not (purchase order, quotation, bank statement, cover letter).
2. Gives per-field rules for the usual traps: vendor is the seller, not "Bill to"; the invoice number is not a PO, GSTIN or order number; the date is the issue
   date, not the due date; ambiguous numeric dates use other clues in the document, then day first for India, UK and Europe and month first for the US.
3. Follows the labelling conventions in the sample pack README: line items are the rows of the item table, `amount` is the row total before tax, charge and discount
   rows of the table are items (discounts negative), `tax_total` is the sum of all tax lines and is 0 when no tax is printed.
4. Says to use `null` rather than guess, and never to do arithmetic to make totals fit.
5. Treats the document as untrusted data, so instructions written inside a document are ignored.

The model also returns `document_type`, `legibility`, `confidence` and `notes`. They feed validation and review flags and are stripped from the public output.
On a retry, the failed checks (with the actual numbers) and the previous output are quoted back.

### 2.4 Validation and what happens when a check fails

| Check | Severity | Action |
| --- | --- | --- |
| JSON parses and matches the schema (ISO dates, 3 letter currency) | error | retry once, else failed record |
| `is_invoice` agrees with `document_type` | error | retry once, else flag |
| vendor, invoice number, date, total and line items present | error | retry once, else flag |
| `sum(line amounts) + tax_total` equals `total`, tolerance 0.05 | error | retry once, else flag |
| total checkable, currency known, date plausible, tax plausible, legibility good, confidence at least 0.7, input quality | warn | flag, no retry |
| scan rated "poor" and the result is untrusted (says "not an invoice", fails an error check, or confidence below 0.7) | failure | failed record `unreadable_document` |

The totals tolerance is 0.05 in currency units. It started at 1.00 to be safe about rounding. The stress test showed a 0.80 misread digit passing under 1.00,
and all 9 labelled invoices and all 17 extracted invoices in the sample pack reconcile to exactly 0.00, so I tightened it. The cost: an invoice that prints a "round off"
line (my prompt creates no item for it) will fail the check and be flagged for review. When the retry does not fix a failure, the record keeps its raw values and
gets `needs_review: true` with the reasons. Bad data is flagged, never silently passed on.

### 2.5 Bad inputs

- **Non-invoices.** `is_invoice: false` and every other field `null`, enforced in code even if the model leaks values. Tested with a purchase order, a bank
  statement and a cover letter that contains a prompt-injection line. On the real pack: L08, U06 (bank statement) and U10 (cover letter) came out correctly.
- **My extra case: damaged or unreadable input, plus API failure.** Missing, empty, oversize, corrupt, truncated, password-protected, blank and unsupported files
  fail before any API call with a specific error code. Timeouts, 429s and 5xx are retried with exponential backoff and jitter. A scan the model itself rates
  "poor", whose result is "not an invoice", fails a check or has low confidence, becomes an `unreadable_document` error record. In every case the result is a
  record with `needs_review: true` and an `error` object, which is deliberately not schema-valid, so a failed read can never pass as a genuine "not an invoice".
  A batch always finishes.
- **What happened on the real sample pack.** None of the 20 documents hit a bad-input path. All were rated legibility "good", none needed a retry, and none was
  flagged. The bad-input paths are covered by tests and, for unreadable scans, by the stress set below.

### 2.6 One thing that did not work

**Retrying on the same model.** A retry only helps if the second read is better than the first. With the stronger model out of daily quota, the retry ran on the
same cheap model. Of the 6 medium-damage scans with misread characters, 4 were retried (the other 2 passed every check) and none became correct: the same documents stayed
wrong, often with the same characters (the year 2026 read as 2020 again). It raised the average cost per stress document from $0.00155 to $0.00204 (+32%) and gave no accuracy gain (15 of 30 before and after).
On severe scans it was worse: in one case the first read came back empty (it failed `core_fields_present`) and the retry returned an invented invoice
(L06_severe came back as "Arvind & Co" with total 5830 instead of "Anand & Rao Associates" with 71673.2, at confidence 0.4). That is what led to the `unreadable_document` rule. The retry never fired on the real pack, so I have
**no measurement that it helps at all**. It stays in the design because its purpose is the stronger model, and I could not test the stronger model as a retry on damaged scans because its free-tier quota ran out during the stress runs.

### 2.7 Overfitting disclosure

I read the sample pack README and looked at the labelled files while designing the prompt conventions and the matching rules, so the 10 of 10 is not independent
of that. But the prompt was **not edited after any eval run**: there is one version, `v1`, hash `26befab048`, and it scored 10 of 10 the first time. Everything
else I changed after seeing results is listed here:

| Change | Made after seeing | Effect on the real pack |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `unreadable_document` rule | stress results (damaged copies of the labelled documents) | none: all 20 real documents were rated "good" |
| totals tolerance 1.00 to 0.05 | one stress document, plus residuals on the real pack | none: every real invoice reconciles to 0.00 |
| free-tier quota fallback, retry logging | the strong model's daily quota running out | none: no retries happened on the real pack |

The stress numbers in section 4 were measured before the tolerance change, and I did not re-run them after it. Both changes were tuned on the stress set, so
the stress numbers are not an independent test of them.

Independent check: I compared all 10 unlabelled outputs against the source documents (the text layer for the 8 digital PDFs, the image itself for U02 and U05),
and found no wrong field. The final release run produced identical JSON for all 10 of them. The arithmetic also reconciles exactly on all 8 invoices, and U04, U09 and the labelled L03 are confirmed by payments on the U06 bank
statement (same invoice numbers and amounts).

## 3. How correctness is measured

Matching rules, implemented in `invoice_extractor/matching.py` and unit tested:

| Field | Rule |
| --- | --- |
| is_invoice | exact boolean |
| vendor | case, accents, punctuation and legal suffixes ignored. Match if equal, or one name's words are a subset of the other's ("Acme" vs "Acme Pvt Ltd"), or similarity at least 0.88 |
| invoice_number | case-insensitive, ignoring spaces and punctuation. Leading zeros matter |
| invoice_date | exact ISO date |
| currency | exact ISO code after mapping symbols |
| tax_total | numeric within 0.01. The pack says no tax is `0`, so `null` does not match `0` |
| total | numeric within 0.01. `null` only matches `null`. `"1,200.00"` equals `1200` |
| line_items | one-to-one item matching: amount within 0.01, quantity equal when the label has one, description similarity at least 0.6. Reported as item F1, amount-only F1 and a strict flag |

Field rows use labelled invoices only, so non-invoices cannot inflate them. `is_invoice` and the whole-document row use all labelled documents. A document that
failed completely counts as wrong on every field. The vendor and line-item rules are the loosest ones and are my judgement calls. Stricter rules would lower those
numbers.

## 5. Failure analysis

Every wrong field in `stress-final` (89 in total), grouped by cause and checked by opening the documents. On the real pack there were no errors to analyse.

| Cause | Fields hit | Docs | Example (expected, got) | Fix |
| --- | ---: | ---: | --- | --- |
| A. Severe damage, not readable | 73 | 10 | L05_severe: all 8 fields lost. Before the fix (`stress-retry`), three severe invoices came back as `is_invoice: false` (confidence 0.2 to 0.9) and one as an invented invoice | **Done.** Now an explicit `unreadable_document` error record. Trade-off: the genuine non-invoice L08_severe is also reported unreadable |
| B. Misread digits and characters on medium scans | 14 (invoice number 4, tax 3, total 3, date 2, line items 2) | 5 | L02_medium: `SGS/1847` read as `SG6/3847`; 2026-08-19 read as 2020-06-18 | Not done: re-read with a stronger model or a higher resolution render, and accept only if two reads agree |
| C. Wrong value that passed every check | 2 | 1 | L10_medium: tax 19688.8 instead of 19689.6, so the sum was 0.80 off, inside the old 1.00 tolerance | **Done.** Tolerance 0.05 (not re-run on the stress set) |

Digital PDFs had no errors anywhere: on the real pack every digital PDF was read from its text layer. All 89 stress errors are on image-only inputs.

**What I would fix first, and why: cause B.** It is the only cause left that returns wrong values without an error record, and it is the biggest source of
wrong invoice numbers and amounts. The concrete change is a second independent read for scans whose legibility is not "good" (different model or a re-rendered
higher resolution page), accepting a value only when two reads agree on invoice number, date, tax and total, and flagging otherwise. I did not build it because
I could not measure the stronger model as a second reader on the stress set within the free-tier quota (20 requests a day), and two reads by the same cheap model repeated the same mistakes (2.6).

**Does the review flag work?** On the real pack it never fired, so the real-data answer is "no evidence either way". On the stress set (`stress-final`) 19 of 30
documents were flagged and 16 had a wrong field. Precision is 79% (15 of 19 flagged documents really had an error), recall is 94% (15 of 16 wrong documents were
flagged), and 98% of wrong fields sit in flagged documents. The one miss is cause C. The signal doing the work is the model's own legibility rating, which fired
on every fair or poor scan. The totals check caught only 2 of the 6 wrong medium documents (L01 and L09): in L02 and L05 the model misread tax and total by the
same amount, so the sum still added up. That is small-sample and synthetic, so read it as anecdotal.

## 6. Cost per document

Method. Every API response carries a `usage` object. For each call, cost is `input_tokens x input price + output_tokens x output price`, using the per-million-token
prices in `pricing.json`. A document's cost is the sum over all its calls, retries and escalations included. Thinking tokens are billed as output and are in the
count. Nothing is estimated from file size or text length. Failed calls that return no tokens cost nothing.

| Set | Documents | Avg cost | Avg tokens in | Avg tokens out | Avg latency |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Labelled pack (the eval) | 10 | $0.00165 | 1,867 | 437 | 8.9 s |
| All 20 documents (`outputs/_run_log.json`) | 20 | $0.00141 | 1,779 | 349 | 14.4 s |
| Stress set, with retries and fallbacks | 30 | $0.00188 | 2,977 | 394 | 8.7 s |

Latency includes the time spent waiting between retries. The final runs hit several Google 503 bursts: U05 took 76 s after four backoff retries and still succeeded,
and the median over all 20 documents is 7.5 s. An earlier run on a quieter API averaged 4.4 s on the labelled pack and 3.5 s on all 20, so treat latency as
dependent on Google's load, not as a property of the pipeline. Tokens and cost do not depend on it, because failed calls return no tokens.

Prices used: Gemini 3.5 Flash-Lite $0.30 input and $2.50 output per million tokens (all first-pass calls). Output tokens are about 60% of the cost. At $0.00141 per
document, 10,000 invoices cost about $14. Running the stronger model on every document instead costs $0.01310 per document (about $131 per 10,000), roughly 8 times the default on the same 10 documents, with the same accuracy. The real bill for this project was $0 because it ran on the free tier. A scanned document costs more than a digital one
(2,245 input tokens for one page image against roughly 1,500 for a text layer).

## 7. Bonus

- **Review flag.** `needs_review` and `review_reasons` come from the failed checks above, the model's own legibility and confidence, and input quality warnings.
  They are kept out of the output JSON because the assignment's `schema.json` forbids extra keys, so they live in `outputs/_run_log.json` (and in each JSON with
  `--with-review`). How well they separate wrong documents from right ones is in section 5.
- **Frontend.** `python -m uvicorn server:app --port 8000`. Upload a document and see it next to the extracted fields, the totals check, the review reasons and the
  cost of each model call.

## 8. Known limitations

- **The totals check is blind to self-consistent mistakes.** A wrong vendor, invoice number or date does not change the arithmetic, and a tax and total misread by the
  same amount still add up. In `stress-final` this left 3 of 6 wrong medium documents flagged only by the model's legibility rating.
- **Round-off lines.** The prompt creates no item for a "round off" line, so an invoice that prints one fails the totals check and is flagged for review. None of the
  20 pack documents has one.
- **Conventions come from the pack README, but two edges are ambiguous.** A total shown after an advance or payment, and adjustments printed only in the totals
  block rather than as table rows. Both are handled as stated in the prompt. I did not see either in the pack.
- **Tax-inclusive line prices** make the totals check fail, because tax is counted twice. These get flagged for review rather than fixed.
- **Ambiguous dates** such as 03/04/2026 rely on context. The prompt defaults to day first outside the US and records the assumption in `notes`.
- **Multi-page limits.** Only the first 6 pages are rendered to images, and a `truncated_pages` warning flags longer documents.
- **Small sample.** 10 labelled documents means one document is 10 points. The stress set is 30 synthetic documents made from those same 10.
- **The stronger-model retry is unmeasured.** The stronger model alone scored 10 of 10 at about 8 times the cost, but the retry path never fired on the real pack, and
  I could not test whether it fixes the misreads in damaged scans, because the free-tier quota ran out during the stress runs.
- **Non-determinism.** Model output varies between runs, and so do Google's outages (several 503 bursts during my runs). The pipeline retries them, but numbers can
  move by a document or two between runs.
